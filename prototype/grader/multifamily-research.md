# Multifamily research brief (gathered 2026-11-14 from web searches; verify with a lender before relying on numbers)

Source pages are mostly lender/broker marketing, so every figure is "typical", not official. Official programs to confirm:
Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, HUD/FHA, and the borrower's own lender.

## Size ladders (what changes with unit count)
- 1 unit = single family. 2-4 units = still a RESIDENTIAL loan (owner-occupied options exist). 5+ units = COMMERCIAL / multifamily underwriting (based on the property's income, DSCR, not your personal income).
- Section 8 / scattered-site "house pack" (for example 5 or 10 single-family houses bought together) is financed as a PORTFOLIO (blanket) loan or as separate DSCR loans, not as an apartment building.
- Small apartment (5-50 units), mid apartment (50-100+ units), large (100+): bigger sizes move to agency, HUD and CMBS debt, need professional management, and take more reserves and due diligence.

## Loan options
- **Conventional 2-4 unit (owner-occupied)**: low down payment options reported from 5% (2 units) to 10-25% (3-4 units); investor purchases typically 15-25% down. Sources disagree, so show as ranges.
  https://www.lower.com/mortgages/fha-loan/fha-multi-family-loans , https://wealthvieu.com/mortgages/real-estate/what-is-a-multi-family-home/
- **FHA 203(b), 2-4 units, owner-occupied (house hacking)**: 3.5% down with a 580+ score (10% for 500-579); move in within about 60 days and stay at least a year; lenders count about 75% of projected rent from the other units; 3-4 units must pass the FHA "self-sufficiency" test (rent must cover the payment) and need about 3 months of reserves; upfront MIP 1.75% plus annual MIP about 0.55-0.85%; county loan limits apply. VA: 0% down on 2-4 units for eligible veterans (owner-occupied). Down-payment assistance often not allowed above 2 units.
  https://www.neighborsbank.com/learn/fha-multifamily-loans/ , https://neuhausre.com/fha-loan-duplex/
- **FHA 203(k) rehab on 2-4 units** (already an app loan option for homes): buy plus renovate in one loan.
- **Agency (Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac) 5+ units**: stabilized properties, about 80% max LTV, DSCR about 1.20-1.40 (1.25 typical), 90%+ occupancy, 5-30 year terms, up to 30-year amortization, usually non-recourse (with carve-outs). Fannie Small Loan: 5-50 units, about $750K-$5M. Freddie Small Balance Loan: about $1M-$7.5M, DSCR 1.20 top markets to 1.40 small markets. Rates roughly 5.5%-7.5% (Feb-Apr 2026 snapshots).
  https://www.walkerdunlop.com/small-balance/ , https://www.loopnet.com/cre-explained/finance/multifamily-financing
- **HUD / FHA 223(f)** (buy or refinance existing 5+ units): up to about 85% LTV (higher for affordable), DSCR about 1.176-1.20, fixed rate, 35-year term and amortization, non-recourse, assumable, slow (many months) and paperwork heavy, mortgage insurance premium. **HUD 221(d)(4)** is new construction / substantial rehab.
- **Bank / credit-union portfolio loans**: 5-7 year terms (or 5/10/20 year fixed periods), 20-25 year amortization, 65-80% LTV, DSCR 1.25-1.30+, often full recourse, flexible and fast, prepayment penalties common.
- **CMBS (conduit)**: 5-10 year terms, 25-30 year amortization, about 65-75% LTV, DSCR about 1.25, loans of about $2M+, non-recourse, defeasance/yield maintenance prepayment.
- **Bridge / transitional loans**: short (1-3 years) interest-only, for value-add (renovate and lease up), rates about 5.75%-12.75%, 1-3 points, 65-80% of cost; refinance into agency/HUD when stabilized. Includes hard-money loans.
- **DSCR investor loans (no personal income test)**: for 1-4 units and small packages; qualify on rent versus payment, DSCR about 1.0-1.25, 20-25% down, higher rate, 30-year. Section 8 vouchers are accepted by many but not all lenders.
- **Blanket / portfolio (rental package) loans**: one loan over several properties (for example a 5- or 10-house Section 8 pack): minimum loan about $500K or 2-5+ properties, up to about 75-80% LTV, DSCR on the portfolio as a whole (1.0-1.25), ask for release clauses for selling single properties. Case study: ~175 scattered-site units financed on one DSCR portfolio loan.
  https://rentalhomefinancing.com/investment-loan-education-center/how-blanket-mortgages-work-for-financing-single-family-rental-properties.html , https://www.biggerpockets.com/posts/7321951
- **SBA 504 / 7(a)**: only when the owner occupies and operates a business in at least ~51% of the building (mixed-use), not for pure rentals.
- **Seller financing**: seller carries a note (often 10-30% down, interest-only or 20-30 year amortization with a 3-10 year balloon), flexible terms, can be a second mortgage "behind" a bank loan.
- **Assumable loan**: take over the seller's existing (often lower-rate) loan; HUD and many agency loans are assumable; needs lender approval and cash for the gap between price and the loan balance.
- **Subject-to**: buy the property while the seller's mortgage stays in place; due-on-sale clause risk (lender can call the loan), legal risk, not a loan the buyer qualifies for.
- **Master lease / lease option (lease-purchase)**: control the whole building through a multi-year lease with an option to buy, sublease the units and keep the spread, buy later with bank financing; risks are the owner's mortgage and your rent obligation on vacant units.
  https://resimpli.com/blog/creative-financing/ , https://dealmachine.com/blog/creative-seller-financing-for-real-estate-investors
- **Joint venture / syndication / equity partner (GP-LP)**: partners put in the down payment, the sponsor operates and earns an acquisition fee, asset management fee and a share ("promote") after a preferred return (often 6-8%); needs securities-law care (Reg D 506(b)/(c)). (Searches returned little on this: described from general knowledge, flag it as such.)
- **Cash-out refinance / HELOC on existing assets** to fund the down payment (the app's trust / credit line already models this).
- **1031 exchange**: defer tax by exchanging a sold property into the new one (45 days to identify, 180 days to close).
- **Seller credits / price negotiation and earnest money / due diligence deposit**: usually 1-3% of price.

## Operating assumptions (typical)
- Operating expense ratio about 35-45% of effective income for professionally managed conventional buildings (often 45-55% for older buildings and small buildings); the app already splits vacancy, tax, insurance, maintenance, management, so add per-unit figures.
- Vacancy about 5-10% (Section 8 and affordable often 5-8%; small buildings lower).
- Management fee about 4-8% of collected rent for large buildings (more, 8-10%, for small buildings or scattered-site Section 8; plus leasing and inspection fees); an on-site manager for 50+ units.
- Replacement reserves about $250-$500 per unit per year (lenders require it, agencies often $250-$350); maintenance/repairs about $800-$1,200 per unit per year on newer buildings (higher on older).
- Payroll for larger buildings (on-site manager, maintenance tech), utilities the owner pays (common-area water/sewer/trash), landscaping, marketing/turnover costs, legal and accounting, replacement of big items (roofs, HVAC, boilers, parking lots), insurance rising with age/size, property tax reassessment after sale.
- Section 8 specifics: HUD Fair Market Rent / payment standard caps the rent; annual inspections (HQS/NSPIRE) and rent reasonableness tests; housing authority pays the voucher part directly, tenant pays the rest; payment can be late; renew leases yearly; per the Cobb example the FMR 3BR was $2,182 and 4BR $2,605 (FY2026).

## Due diligence checklist (what a buyer collects)
Rent roll and leases, trailing 12-month income and expenses (T-12) and 2-3 years of tax returns/financials, utility bills, estoppel certificates, property tax bill and reassessment estimate, insurance quotes and loss runs, physical condition assessment (PCA), roof/HVAC/plumbing/electrical, Phase I environmental, survey/title, zoning and certificate of occupancy for the unit count, unit-by-unit inspection (sample), capital-needs list, deferred maintenance, management contract, service contracts, tenant mix and delinquency, comparable rents and sales (market study), seller financing / assumption documents.
