A new way to add and view assets

Each category on the main page now opens to two buttons. View assets shows every home with its numbers on one small card, and Add an asset walks you through swipeable question cards, about 19 for a typical home. Three finished designs, picked from 21 tested.

Every question card, in order

32 cards in six sections: Basics, Price & loan, Fix-up, Income, Costs, Plan, then a review. Cards marked "only if" appear only when an earlier answer calls for them, so a typical planned home is 19 cards. Most come pre-filled with your defaults or a typical value, and Skip keeps it.

Multi-familyAdds "How many units?" after the first card (and a number box for 5 or more), "Rent per unit" in place of the rent card, drops beds and baths, and offers 2 to 4 unit, FHA, DSCR and apartment loans.
CommercialRent becomes "Lease income", the home and HOA cards drop out, a "Who pays taxes and insurance?" card asks for the NNN share, and the loans are bank commercial, SBA 504, DSCR or cash.
BusinessRent, tax, insurance, HOA, repairs and the manager card are replaced by "Yearly revenue" and "Profit margin", and the loans are SBA 7(a), bank term, seller note or cash.
OwnedAdds "What is it worth today?" and "How much is left on the loan?", skips closing costs, and "When should it be bought?" is replaced by "When did you buy it?".

How the designs were picked

Six designs per screen went through a grading program that scores informative, small, friendly, intuitive, pretty and research fit (plus typing for the cards). Round 1 best: M4 95, L2 81.5, C5 98.7. Each winner was then rebuilt to fix what cost it points (the 7th design on each screen). An independent review then found real bugs the grader missed, so the grader was made stricter (it now checks the shared styles, colour contrast in all six themes, tap width, whether labels fit at phone width, whether every claim matches the screen, and whether the question order keeps its promises) and the designs were fixed again. Scores below are from that stricter round. The grader is a program that reads each design's code, so "pretty" means checks like theme colours only, five type sizes or fewer, no text under 12px and theme corners, not taste.

Because a program can't judge taste, a separate reviewer that hadn't built the designs also used and scored the three finals after each fix round, out of 100 (main page, list, cards). Over 15 rounds the scores went from 86 / 80 / 78 to 91 / 90 / 90, every page at the 90 bar. Every bug those reviews found was fixed, including three small ones the last review listed, and all of it is covered by 162 automated tap tests that pass.

What changes in the app

  1. Each category dropdown (Homes, Commercial, Businesses, owned and planned) shows a one-line summary and two buttons, Add an asset and View assets, in place of the rows of assets.
  2. A new View assets screen per category lists every asset as a card with the numbers above, a sort row, and Add at the top. Tapping a home's name opens today's asset page, unchanged (here it says so, since the prototype has no asset page).
  3. Add an asset opens a full-screen card deck in place of today's add form. Answers fill the same fields the form fills today, so the engine and money math do not change.
  4. Starting from Homes, the first card only asks single-family or multi-family; from Commercial or Businesses it is skipped. Pasting a listing link on the address card pre-fills what the listing has, the way listing import does today.
  5. The review card shows the result and the "Can I buy this?" verdict before anything is saved. Every row has Edit, which jumps back to that card.

Things to note

  • Every (i) that explains a term or number stays, per your popup rule. New ones: cap rate, DSCR, cash-on-cash, loan types, repairs reserve.
  • Months until the trust can afford it comes from the same funding month the plan already computes ("opens in N mo" today). Planned homes are funded in buy order, so the second one starts saving after the first is bought. The sample trust here holds $41,000 and adds $3,150 a month.
  • Totals on the main page and the list count owned homes only, so planned homes never look like money you already have.
  • The interest rate is on the loan card, as you described: pick a loan and the card stays, showing its rate (pre-filled with today's typical rate) and loan length, with the monthly payment. Next moves on. Picking Cash moves on by itself, and Change loan brings the list back. Typed answers are kept when you go back, and "Per year" on tax and insurance is turned into a monthly figure.
  • Research says one-question flows feel long past 12 to 14 questions, so the deck groups cards into sections, pre-fills typical values, offers Skip on every card, and auto-advances on picks. A planned home with no rehab or HOA takes 19 cards, about two minutes.
  • Swiping also works with Back and Next buttons and the arrow keys, for anyone who does not swipe.
  • All numbers here are sample numbers, worked out with simple math (5% vacancy, 1% repairs). The app keeps its own full math.
  • Your Oxford theme's text stroke makes 12 to 13px labels look a little heavy, and in Tech Noir the accent is light grey, so filled buttons read as neutral. Both are theme settings you chose, so I left them; say if you want small labels to skip the stroke.
  • I couldn't make PNG screenshots: no browser in my workspace can render pages tonight. This page shows the real designs instead, live and at phone size, in all six of your themes.
Research read for this design
Per-home numbers: DealCheck, how to analyze rentals (input order: purchase and financing, then rent and expenses; formulas behind a ? icon) · Baselane property metrics (value, NOI, cash-on-cash, cap rate) · RentRedi portfolio performance (roll-up, then per-property breakdown) · Lendlord portfolio.
Question cards: Smashing, one thing per page · Fillout, one question at a time (auto-advance on choices; long flows past 12 to 14 questions hurt) · TinyCommand, card mode forms (honest progress, always a back option).
