## 3. Signing up and signing in (free; no codes to join)
Two ways, both free:
1. **Email + password.** "Create an account": name, email, password (12 characters or more, checked against a short list of common passwords in the app; Supabase's own leaked-password check is a paid-plan feature, confirm in ACC0). The account works at once; nothing is emailed.
2. **Continue with Google.** Free Google Cloud OAuth client (a free sign-up, his yes) with the app's address as the redirect and its consent screen published to production (in test mode only listed test users can sign in). A new Google account is added automatically, no approval.
LATER, each his yes: Apple (Apple Developer Program, "99 USD per membership year", developer.apple.com/programs/enroll, read Nov 25; needed for the App Store anyway) and phone (texts cost money and would break "never texts you").

Enforced on the server: a Supabase "Before User Created" auth hook (`hook_before_user_created`, a Postgres function, written and tested) runs before any user is made and refuses:
- any provider but email and Google;
- a password sign-up with a gmail.com or googlemail.com address ("For a Gmail address, tap Continue with Google."), so nobody can take someone's Gmail address with a password before they arrive;
- every sign-up while the Owner has "Allow new accounts" off (on by default).
A trigger then makes the person's `app_accounts` row: active, Member, the Owner's default switches. Clients can't insert, delete or change protected columns (section 6). A sign-in email can never change once set (a trigger on Supabase's user table refuses it; to use a new email, make a new account), so the Gmail rule can't be dodged by signing up with another address and switching to a Gmail one, and the account list never goes stale.
Supabase itself answers a sign-up for an email that already has an account with "already registered". The app shows "Couldn't create that account. If it's yours, sign in." That still tells someone the email has an account; it can't be hidden without sending email, which he ruled out.

Same email both ways: Supabase links a Google sign-in to an existing account with the same email, and with "Confirm email" off it treats that email as verified. Someone could make a password account at a non-Gmail address that belongs to a person who later uses Google with it (a company Google account). A trigger (`on_identity_linked`, written and tested) closes that: the moment a Google identity joins an account that has a password, the password is removed, every old session and saved sign-in is signed out, the history logs it and the Owner screen marks the account. From then on only that Google account signs in to it, and the app asks the person to check their name. Whether Supabase lets the trigger run inside its linking step is confirmed in ACC0; if it doesn't, owner-admin finds every account that has both a password and Google each time the Owner screen loads, and removes the password there (a short window, not none). A removed password can't be put back (a trigger, tested).

Forgotten password (v7, his words: "they should have a temp password sent to their email and when they login with the temp password they should be prompted to update their password"):
1. "Forgot password?" on the sign-in page asks for the email and says "If there's an account with a password for that email, a temporary password is on its way. It works once, for 15 minutes." It gives that same answer whether or not the account exists, when a request comes too soon after the last one, and when sending fails, so this screen doesn't tell anyone which emails have accounts (sign-up's "already registered" message still can; that is accepted). A gmail.com or googlemail.com address gets "Use Continue with Google" instead (those can only be Google accounts). Google accounts never get a password (trigger, tested).
2. The email holds an 8-digit temporary password (Supabase's recovery code, sent through his Gmail). The sign-in page shows a "Temporary password" box after a request, and the ordinary password box also accepts it: if a password sign-in fails and the entry is 8 digits, the app tries it as the temporary password (verifyOtp, type recovery).
3. Signing in with it goes straight to "Set a new password" (12 or more characters, typed twice). The server enforces this too: a session made from an emailed code (Supabase marks it recovery, otp or magiclink) is not a full sign-in (`full_signin()`), so it can't read or change its own account row, read notices, or, for the Owner, read accounts or history or change anything (tested). delete-me also refuses it. Setting the password clears `must_change_password` (trigger, tested); the app then signs in again with the new password and signs the code session out. Closing the app before that leaves them signed out. Right after the code is accepted, before "Set a new password", the app reads the account's sign-in methods (Supabase's identities); if Google is among them it says "This account signs in with Google" and signs out (the trigger would refuse a password anyway). ACC1a tests this with a Google-linked account at a non-Gmail address.
4. The old password keeps working until a new one is set, so asking for someone else's temporary password can't lock them out. Requests are limited by Supabase (one per address every 5 minutes and 10 emails an hour in all, set in ACC0), and each one is written to the history, with no address or code (trigger on Supabase's "recovery sent" time, tested).
5. Signed in on a device with a passkey, Settings > Sign-in > "Change password" (asks the passkey first) still works with no email.
6. The Owner screen's button is "Email a temporary password" (owner-admin sends the same reset email and sets `must_change_password`), for when someone asks Austin for help. It shows only for accounts whose sign-in methods (Supabase's identities, not the provider column) include no Google. He doesn't hand the password on, but every email sent through his Gmail, code and address included, keeps a copy in his Gmail Sent folder (section 12).

## 4. The sign-in page on every open
His words: prompted with a sign-in page whenever the app is opened. Every cold open, and coming back after 15 minutes in the background, shows the sign-in page before any portfolio is read from storage.
- First time on a device: app name, the privacy line (section 8), "Continue with Google", email and password boxes with "Sign in", and "Create an account". Wrong password: "That email and password don't match." with no hint whether the email exists; 5 tries, then 60 seconds' wait (plus Supabase's own limits).
- After a full sign-in on a device: one card, "Sign in with a passkey next time?", with "Use a passkey" and "Not now". A passkey uses the device's own lock: Face ID or Touch ID, Windows Hello, or the device PIN (WebAuthn platform key, user verification required). "Not now" means password or Google on every open; Settings > Sign-in can turn the passkey on or off later.
- What the passkey protects: the saved sign-in is kept only encrypted (AES-GCM) with a key the passkey itself produces (WebAuthn's PRF extension), so without the passkey the saved sign-in can't be used even by someone reading the device's storage. Where the browser or claude.ai can't give that key, "Use a passkey" isn't offered there and password or Google is the default, as he asked (which browsers do is confirmed in ACC0).
- Owner changes need a fresh sign-in: changing switches, suspending, sending or taking back a notice, emailing a temporary password and closing new accounts all need a full password or Google sign-in in the last 15 minutes. The app asks "Confirm it's you" first; the database checks the sign-in time in Supabase's token (`owner_fresh()`, tested), so a stolen saved sign-in can read (every account's email and history, which the plan accepts for now) but can't change anything.
- With a passkey on this device: "Welcome back" (the name shows only after the passkey passes) and "Continue with passkey", with "Use my password" under it. The passkey unlocks the saved sign-in kept on this device, then the app refreshes it with Supabase. If Supabase's own passkey sign-in is available when ACC0 runs, that is used instead (confirm in ACC0), and its sign-in method name is added to `full_signin()` so passkey users keep access to their own row and notices.
- A full sign-in is needed again on a new device, after signing out, 30 days after the last full sign-in (checked by the app; Supabase's own session limits are "only available on Pro Plans and up", supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/sessions), after 5 failed passkey tries, or when the Owner suspends the account (the next refresh is refused).
- Offline (no signal, or the free project paused): "Continue with passkey" still opens that person's own data on that device, shows "Offline: changes stay on this device", and uses the permissions from the last online sign-in, for up to 30 days after the last online sign-in. Without a passkey, offline means no sign-in on that open; the page says so.
- Sign out: menu item. "Sign out and remove my data from this device" is a second, confirmed option that saves a backup file first.
- Restore from backup lives behind a signed-in session (Settings > Backups), not on the sign-in page.
- Honest limit: the sign-in step stops someone who picks up the phone. It doesn't stop a person with developer tools on an unlocked computer, because the portfolio is stored unencrypted on the device, as today (section 9).

## 6. Accounts, roles, permissions and notices
Database (written: acc-prototype/supabase/migrations/0001_accounts.sql and 0002_bootstrap_owner.sql):
- `app_accounts`: uid, display_name, email, provider (email | google), role (owner | member), perms (only the known switches, each true or false), status (active | suspended), require_setup, must_change_password, password_removed_at, created_at. No portfolio data. A Member's name can't include the word "Owner", at sign-up or later; the Owner screen always shows the sign-in email beside a name. Named so it doesn't clash with Portfolio.accounts[].
- `account_events`: every sign-up and change, before and after, written by triggers; only the Owner reads it.
- `app_settings`: one row, "Allow new accounts" and the default switches.
- `notices`, `notice_recipients`, `notice_reads` (below).

Roles now: **Owner** (Austin only; every switch; can't be removed, suspended or demoted) and **Member** (everyone else, starting with the default switches). Admin and Viewer stay LATER, as presets of the same switches.
Permission switches per account: Can sign in (suspend or restore, through owner-admin); Add and edit assets; Edit Settings and Finances; Import and export; Saved portfolios; Learn; Must redo setup on next sign-in.

Who can change what, enforced in the database:
1. Bootstrap: Austin makes his account first (ACC0); migration 0002 then defines `owner_uid()` as his id and makes his row Owner. Only a new migration can change that.
2. Rows are made only by the sign-up trigger. Clients have no insert or delete right.
3. Row rules (RLS): a person reads their own row; the Owner reads all. Updates: a person on their own row while active, the Owner on any row.
4. Column guard (`guard_app_accounts`, a trigger, because RLS can't limit columns): a Member may change only their display name; the Owner may change perms, require_setup and names, never status, email, provider or dates, and never make anyone else Owner; only owner-admin (the service key) changes status and last sign-in; nobody changes the Owner's own role, status or switches.
5. Status goes through one Owner-only Edge Function, `owner-admin`: it first calls `owner_fresh()` with the caller's own token (so it is the Owner with a full sign-in in the last 15 minutes; otherwise it answers "Confirm it's you", tested), then with the service key bans or unbans the Supabase user (admin API ban duration, confirm in ACC0) and sets `status` in the same call, so sign-in and refresh stop on the server. It also does Email a temporary password (sends the reset email and sets `must_change_password`; not offered for Google accounts, which the database keeps password-free), Remove account (deletes the user; the row and reads go with it) and reads last sign-in times from Supabase's own records (the account row keeps none). The Owner's own sign-in user and row can't be deleted at all (triggers, tested).
6. A suspended person can still open their own data offline with a passkey on a device they already used, until 30 days after their last online sign-in. That reaches only their own device data.
7. "Delete my account" (Settings > Account, everyone but the Owner; a downloadable app must have it, and next.md P1.5 had it): a confirmed step that offers a backup file of their device data, then a small `delete-me` Edge Function deletes their own Supabase user with the service key (checking the caller's token is that user and a full sign-in, not a temporary-password session). Their row, notice reads and sign-ins go; the history keeps only a bare "account deleted" line with the opaque id (tested). Then the device data is removed.
The app reads the person's row at every online sign-in and locks screens to match. Nobody, Austin included, can see another person's portfolio, and the Owner screen says so.

Owner screen (Settings > Accounts, Owner only): search; every account as a card (name, sign-in email, Google or password, status, last sign-in), newest first, with a "New" tag for a week; tapping one opens its switches, "Suspend", "Ask to redo setup", "Email a temporary password" (accounts with no Google sign-in), "Remove account" (confirmed; their device data is theirs) and its change history. Cards for "Default switches for new accounts", "Allow new accounts" (on), keep-alive status, and "Send a notice".

Notices (his answer 5; notices never go to anyone's email):
- Owner screen > "Send a notice": title (up to 80 characters), message (up to 2,000, plain text), "Everyone" or "Choose people" (search and tick; each person shown with name and sign-in email, since names aren't unique), a preview, then "Send" with a confirm that says how many people get it. Sent notices are listed with how many have read them, and "Take back" removes one.
- Everyone else: a bell on the top bar with an unread count, and a Notices page (newest first, tap to read, marked read). They're fetched at each online sign-in and when the app comes back to the screen. While a notice is unread, a one-line banner shows on the first page once.
- A notice for everyone reaches the people who had an account when it was sent; people who join later don't get old ones.
- Database rules (tested): only the Owner, with a fresh sign-in, can send or take back; a notice for chosen people is seen only by them; nobody sees who else got one; a person can mark only their own read and only on notices they can see; suspended or signed-out people see none. Text is shown as plain text, never as page code.
- Push notifications to the phone's lock screen come only with the downloadable app, his yes then.
